\addchap{Abstract}

Axions and axionlike particles (ALPs) are well-motivated candidates for the dark matter that makes up most of the matter content of the universe.
In the nuclear-gradient interaction, an ALP dark-matter field acts as an effective oscillating pseudomagnetic field on nuclear spins, and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) provides a resonant method for detecting this weak drive.

This thesis develops the signal models, frequency-scanning strategy, and data-analysis framework for ALP searches with the Cosmic Axion Spin Precession Experiment (CASPEr), and applies them to proof-of-principle measurements with the CASPEr-Gradient-LowField apparatus.

Two ALP dark-matter models are considered: a virialized Milky Way halo, which gives a stochastic signal with finite coherence time and a linewidth set by the velocity distribution, and a halo of ALPs gravitationally bound to Earth, which can produce long-lived, narrowband, and direction-dependent signals with potentially enhanced local dark-matter density.

For frequency scans, 2 regimes are identified according to the relative magnitudes of the NMR coherence time $T_2^*$ and the ALP coherence time $\tau_a$.
In the long-measurement limit, the coupling sensitivity improves only as $T_\mathrm{tot}^{-1/4}$ with total integration time in both regimes.
To maximize the sensitivity, the relaxation time $T_2^*$ should be as long as possible, and the frequency step size should equal the sensitive bandwidth of a single measurement.

The experimental chapter describes the CASPEr-Gradient-LowField apparatus used in this thesis, including the superconducting magnet system, the methanol sample, the pickup coil, the SQUID detection chain, and the Python-based control and acquisition software.
The sample consists of thermally polarized liquid methanol at about \SI{180}{\kelvin}, with a proton polarization of only \num{1.8e-7}, which is the dominant limitation on sensitivity.

The data-analysis pipeline includes time-series sanity checks, Fourier transformation, filter correction, baseline removal, normalized-power-excess thresholding, and outlier validation.
The Milky Way ALP search scanned a band of
\begin{equation}
    \SI{1.348570}{\MHz}\pm\SI{120}{\Hz}
    \,\nonumber
\end{equation}
in three sequential scans with \SI{100}{\s} acquisitions at each frequency step.
No statistically significant candidate survived the cross-scan consistency checks, yielding a \SI{95}{\percent} confidence-level limit of $|g_\mathrm{ap}| \lesssim \SI{1e-2}{\GeV^{-1}}$ over the scanned mass range of
\begin{equation}
    \SI{5.577237}{\neV/c^2}\pm\SI{496}{\femto\eV/c^2}\,.\nonumber
\end{equation}
The Earth-bound ALP search scanned
\begin{equation}
    \SI{1.348575}{\MHz}\pm\SI{24}{\Hz}
    \,\nonumber
\end{equation}
with a measurement duration of \SI{1}{\hour} at each of 8 frequency steps.
No statistically significant candidate was found, giving an approximate \SI{95}{\percent} confidence-level limit of $|g_\mathrm{ap}| \lesssim \SI{1e-9}{\GeV^{-1}}$ over the scanned mass range of
\begin{equation}
    \SI{5.577258}{\neV/c^2} \pm \SI{99}{\femto\electronvolt/c^2}
    \,\nonumber
\end{equation}
under the Earth-bound halo assumptions used in the analysis.

These measurements demonstrate the use of liquid-state NMR to search for ALP-proton gradient coupling and establish an experimental and analytical basis for more sensitive searches with hyperpolarized samples across different ALP halo models.

The most promising upgrades are hyperpolarized samples, lower noise, better field homogeneity, and larger sample volume.
Hyperpolarized methanol could improve the projected coupling sensitivity by orders of magnitude, while highly polarized liquid \ch{^{129}Xe} with long transverse relaxation times offers a further route to improved sensitivity.
The numerical Bloch-dynamics simulator and statistical framework developed here support the analysis of diverse dark-matter models.
These developments could broaden the reach of CASPEr to axion streams, dark photons, and other new-physics scenarios.

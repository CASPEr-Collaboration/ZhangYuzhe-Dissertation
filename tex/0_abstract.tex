\addchap{Abstract}

Axions and axionlike particles (ALPs) are well-motivated candidates for the dark matter that makes up most of the matter content of the universe.
In the nuclear-gradient interaction, an ALP dark-matter field acts as an effective oscillating pseudomagnetic field on nuclear spins, and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) provides a resonant method for detecting this weak drive.

This thesis develops the signal models, frequency-scanning strategy, and data-analysis framework for ALP searches with the Cosmic Axion Spin Precession Experiment (CASPEr), and applies them to proof-of-principle measurements with the CASPEr-Gradient-Lowfield apparatus.

Two dark-matter scenarios are considered: a virialized Milky Way halo, which gives a stochastic signal with finite coherence time and a linewidth set by the velocity distribution, and gravitationally bound Earth-bound states, which can produce long-lived, narrow, and direction-dependent signals with potentially enhanced local dark-matter density.
For frequency scans, four regimes are identified according to the ordering of the NMR coherence time $T_2^*$, the intrinsic relaxation time $T_2$, and the ALP coherence time $\tau_a$.
In all long-measurement-duration regimes, the coupling sensitivity improves only as $T_\mathrm{tot}^{-1/4}$ with total integration time, so better polarization, smaller field inhomogeneity, longer coherence times, and lower detector noise are more effective than simply measuring longer.
The optimal frequency step to maximize the sensitivity is the sensitive bandwidth of a single measurement.

The experimental chapter describes the CASPEr-Gradient-Lowfield apparatus used in this thesis, including the superconducting magnet system, the methanol sample, the pickup coil, the SQUID detection chain, and the Python-based control and acquisition software.
The measured sample is thermally polarized liquid methanol at about \SI{180}{\kelvin}, with a very small proton polarization of approximately \SI{1.8e-7}{}, which sets the dominant sensitivity limitation.

The data-analysis pipeline includes time-series sanity checks, Fourier transformation, lock-in response correction, Savitzky--Golay baseline removal, normalized-power-excess thresholding, and candidate validation.
The Milky Way ALP search scanned a \SI{240}{\Hz} band around \SI{1.348570}{\MHz} in three sequential scans with \SI{100}{\s} acquisitions at each frequency step.
Optimal filtering with the expected ALP lineshape was used to search for the stochastic signal.
No statistically significant candidate survived the cross-scan consistency checks, yielding a \SI{95}{\percent} confidence-level limit of $|g_\mathrm{ap}| \lesssim \SI{1e-2}{\GeV^{-1}}$ over the scanned mass range near \SI{5.577237}{\neV/c^2}.
The Earth-bound ALP search scanned a \SI{48}{\Hz} band around \SI{1.348575}{\MHz}, using \SI{1}{\hour} acquisitions at each of eight frequency steps.
An unresolved one-bin statistic replaced the optimal-filter convolution to target signals narrower than the spectral resolution bandwidth.
No statistically significant candidate was found, giving an approximate \SI{95}{\percent} confidence-level limit of $|g_\mathrm{ap}| \lesssim \SI{1e-9}{\GeV^{-1}}$ over the scanned range under the Earth-bound halo assumptions used in the analysis.

The most promising upgrades are hyperpolarized samples, improved temperature control, lower noise, better field homogeneity, larger sample volume, and enhanced pickup performance.
Hyperpolarized methanol could improve the projected coupling sensitivity by up to 6 orders of magnitude, while highly polarized liquid \ch{129Xe} with long transverse relaxation times offers a further route to improved sensitivity.
The numerical Bloch-dynamics simulator and statistical framework developed here support wider frequency scans and comparisons between dark-matter models, spin species, and coupling structures.
These developments could broaden the reach of CASPEr to axion streams, dark photons, and other new-physics scenarios.

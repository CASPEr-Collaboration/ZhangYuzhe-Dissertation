The research presented in this thesis was carried out within the CASPEr collaboration. 
My contributions included experimental work, theoretical calculations, simulations, data analysis, and the design and optimization of search strategies.

For the frequency-scanning study\,\cite{Yuzhe2023_FrequencyScanning}, I performed all theoretical calculations and analysis, wrote the initial manuscript, and coordinated the work within the collaboration.
I was the first and corresponding author.

For the theoretical study of Earth-bound ALPs, I contributed to the development of the theoretical model and numerically solved the Schrödinger equation for the gravitationally bound states in the Earth's potential.

I contributed to the construction and commissioning of the apparatus used for the experimental searches for Milky Way halo ALPs and Earth-bound ALPs.

For the Milky Way halo ALP search\,\cite{Walter2025_CASPErLFthermal}, I contributed to data acquisition, analytical calculations, data analysis, manuscript preparation, and coordination within the collaboration.
I was one of three co-first authors who contributed equally.

For the Earth-bound ALP search, I contributed to data acquisition, analytical calculations, data analysis, manuscript preparation, and coordination within the collaboration.

I designed and implemented the \texttt{axionbloch} software for simulating axion-induced spin dynamics\,\cite{Zhang2026_axionbloch,Zhang2026_repoAxionBloch}.

The publications and manuscripts I authored or coauthored are listed at the end of this thesis. 
The apparatus and the broader CASPEr research program reflect the work of many collaborators; the statements above identify my individual contributions to the specified projects.

\section{Conclusions}

In this thesis, a search for axionlike-particle dark matter through the nuclear-gradient coupling using nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy is described.
In this interaction, the spatial gradient of the ALP field acts as an oscillating pseudomagnetic field on nuclear spins.
When the Larmor frequency of the sample is tuned to the ALP Compton frequency, this weak drive can generate a transverse magnetization that is detected by the CASPEr-Gradient-LowField apparatus.

In the theoretical work, the signal models and the data analysis framework needed for such a search were developed.
Two dark-matter scenarios were considered.
For virialized Milky Way halo ALPs, the field is a stochastic superposition of many velocity modes, giving a finite coherence time and a linewidth set by the standard halo model. 
For Earth-bound ALPs, the field is instead described by bound states in the Earth's gravitational potential, leading to discrete eigenstates, longer coherence times, and a potentially higher dark matter density than that of the virialized halo.

The frequency-scanning analysis identified four signal regimes determined by the ordering of $T_2^*$, $T_2$, and the ALP coherence time $\tau_a$.
In all long-measurement-duration regimes considered here, the coupling sensitivity scales only as $T_\mathrm{tot}^{-1/4}$ with total measurement time.
% This slow scaling makes the main experimental lesson clear: improving spin polarization, field homogeneity, $T_2^*$, $T_2$, and detector noise is more powerful than simply accumulating longer data sets.
To maximize the sensitivity, the relaxation times $T_2^*$ and $T_2$ should be maximized, regardless of whether a narrow or broad frequency band is being searched.
The optimal frequency step size equals the sensitive bandwidth of a single measurement.

In the experimental work, this framework was applied to proof-of-principle measurements with thermally polarized methanol.

The December 23, 2022 Milky Way ALP search scanned a frequency band of $\SI{1.348570}{\MHz}\pm \SI{120}{\Hz}$, corresponding to ALP masses
$\SI{5.577237}{\neV/c^2}\pm\SI{496}{\femto\eV/c^2}$.
Three sequential scans were performed, with \SI{100}{\s} ALP-search acquisitions at each frequency step.
The data were processed through sanity checks, Fourier transformation, lock-in response correction, Savitzky--Golay baseline removal, optimal filtering with the expected ALP lineshape, and normalized-power-excess thresholding. 
No statistically significant Milky Way ALP candidate survived the cross-scan consistency checks.
The resulting \SI{95}{\percent} confidence-level constraint is
\begin{equation}
    |g_\mathrm{ap}| \lesssim \SI{1e-2}{\GeV^{-1}}\,
\end{equation}
over the scanned mass range.
This narrow-band search serves as a commissioning measurement for CASPEr-Gradient-LowField and, to the best of our knowledge, is the first dedicated laboratory search in this mass range.

The December 14, 2022 Earth-bound ALP measurement used the same apparatus and sample but employed a longer measurement duration.
It scanned $\SI{1.348575}{\MHz}\pm\SI{24}{\Hz}$ with \SI{1}{\hour} no-pulse acquisitions at each of eight scan steps, targeting the narrower linewidth and longer coherence time expected for Earth-bound ALP states.
This frequency range corresponds to ALP masses $\SI{5.577258}{\neV/c^2}\pm\SI{99}{\femto\eV/c^2}$.
The analysis framework for this data set was structured analogously to that of the Milky Way search, but a one-bin statistic for unresolved signals replaced the optimal-filter convolution because the ALP linewidth is smaller than the resolution bandwidth.
No statistically significant Earth-bound ALP candidate was found, and the resulting \SI{95}{\percent} confidence-level constraint is approximately \SI{1e-9}{\GeV^{-1}} over the scanned range. 

The significance of these measurements is the demonstration of liquid-state NMR as an experimental method for searching for ALP-proton gradient coupling.
The Milky Way ALP search establishes the operation of the CASPEr-Gradient-LowField apparatus and its analysis procedure as a commissioning measurement\,\cite{Walter2025_CASPErLFthermal}.
This thesis extends that approach to search for Earth-bound ALPs.
Together, these contributions provide an experimental and analytical basis for future searches with hyperpolarized samples and for interpreting their results under different ALP halo assumptions.

\section{Outlook}

The most direct path to improved sensitivity is higher nuclear-spin molar polarization.
The thermally polarized methanol sample used here has a proton polarization of only approximately $\num{1.8e-7}$, which limits the sensitivity.
The apparatus already uses a highly sensitive SQUID magnetometer, so the very small sample magnetization makes increased polarization a particularly promising route to substantial gains\,\cite{Walter2025_CASPErLFthermal}.
Further improvements in detection are possible: resonant pickup circuits can improve inductive readout, as illustrated by the quantum-limited measurements of Kuenstner and collaborators\,\cite{Kuenstner2026_QuantumMetrology}, while spin squeezing could reduce spin-projection noise in a suitable measurement quadrature\,\cite{Boyers2025_SpinSqueezing}.
These approaches require careful control of circuit losses, back-action, and spin dynamics; spin squeezing does not directly remove the electronic noise of the present detector.
They therefore represent demanding, complementary developments alongside hyperpolarization.
Hyperpolarized samples, produced for example by brute-force prepolarization, parahydrogen-induced polarization, or dynamic nuclear polarization, could improve the projected coupling sensitivity by up to 6 orders of magnitude.
Liquid \ch{^{129}Xe} polarized by spin-exchange optical pumping offers fractional polarization approaching unity together with a long $T_2$.
Recent progress in \ch{^{129}Xe} hyperpolarization and in the development of the temperature-controlled sample probe for the CASPEr-Gradient-HighField setup demonstrates the potential to improve the sensitivity of CASPEr-Gradient-LowField.

Further gains should come from longer coherence in the sample and a quieter, more homogeneous apparatus.
Improving the bias-field homogeneity from the present \SI{10}{ppm} scale toward the projected \SI{2}{ppm} level would increase both $T_2^*$ and the fraction of spins resonant with a narrow ALP signal.
The LF setup is also expected to benefit from an upgraded variable-temperature insert (VTI), which should improve temperature stability and operating flexibility.
Lower electronic noise, larger effective sample volume and improved pickup-coil performance would enhance the sensitivity.
These improvements are complementary: polarization increases the available magnetization, while long relaxation times increase the accumulated ALP-induced tipping angle.

The Earth-bound ALP analysis opens a second direction for future work.
Because bound states can have long coherence times and geometry-dependent gradients, the optimal statistic, signal normalization, and validation differ from those used for the Milky Way halo.
Completing this analysis requires finalizing the state-population assumptions, the reference signal power, the recovery efficiency, and the candidate consistency criteria.
The transient jumps seen in the one-hour records should also be catalogued systematically.
They are likely instrumental in origin, but the same tools may become useful for searches for transient exotic signals such as ALP domain walls.

Finally, the numerical and statistical framework developed here can be extended beyond the specific methanol measurements.
The \texttt{axionbloch} simulations provide a way to test Bloch dynamics for realistic relaxation, field inhomogeneity, hyperpolarized $T_1$ decay, and stochastic ALP fields.
% The Neyman--Pearson and optimal-filter treatment provides a general basis for future search statistics.
Together with the broader frequency reach of CASPEr-Gradient-LowField and the HighField apparatus, these tools prepare CASPEr for wider scans of ALP parameter space and for comparisons between different dark-matter models, spin species, and coupling structures.
One interesting direction is the dual-NMR-sample concept, which could expand the experimental reach to correlated searches for axions and other new-physics signals such as dark photons\,\cite{dror2025_axiondualNMR}.

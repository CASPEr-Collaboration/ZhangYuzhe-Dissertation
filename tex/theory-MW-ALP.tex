\section{Milky Way halo}\label{sec:theory:MWALP}

\subsection{Virialized dark matter halo}

In the standard halo model (SHM)\,\cite{Evans2019_SHMRefined}, the Milky Way dark matter population is treated as a virialized\,\footnote{The term ``virialized'' means that the dark matter is gravitationally bound. } ensemble. 
% The gravitational potential constrains the dark matter to a roughly spherical halo. 
In the Galactic rest frame, the dark-matter velocity distribution is approximated by a Maxwell--Boltzmann distribution truncated near the Galactic escape speed \cite{Evans2019_SHMRefined}.\footnote{The velocity dispersion characterizes the width of the distribution; it is not another name for an individual velocity.}
The average speed of local dark matter\footnote{Here, ``local'' refers to the solar radius in the Milky Way.} is $v_\mathrm{DM} \approx \SI{220}{\km\per\s}$.
The local dark matter density is estimated to be $\rho_{DM}\approx\SI{0.4}{\GeV\per\centi\meter^3}$. 

Though it is ``standard'' halo model, it is not a perfect description of the Milky Way dark matter halo. It is more of a model built on phenomenological observations, rather than a model tested through direct measurements. 
Not every question about the Milky Way halo can be answered by the SHM. For example, the SHM does not account for the presence of substructure in galaxies. 
There have been alternative models or modifications to the SHM, including SHM$^{++}$\,\cite{Evans2019_SHMRefined}, caustic rings\,\footnote{$Sikivie caustic rings$}, and the presence of substructure in the form of streams\,\footnote{$Freese2004_Streams$, $Savage2006_Streams$, $Kuhlen2010_Streams$, $Lisanti2011_Streams$}. 
This thesis focuses on the SHM for simplicity. 
In the future, CASPEr can explore more complex models of the dark matter halo.

\subsection{Stochasticity due to inteference}\label{sec:theory:MWALP:stochasticity}

If the ALP constitutes the dominant component of the Milky Way dark matter, the local ALP field is a superposition of many modes with different velocities and phases. 
The relative spread of velocities is on the order of $v_a/c \sim 10^{-3}$.
Due to the second-order Doppler effect, the relative spread of kinetic energies is on the order of $(v_a/c)^2 \sim 10^{-6}$. 


so the ALP field is approximately monochromatic, with a fractional linewidth $\Delta\nu_a/\nu_a \sim 10^{-6}$.


A terrestrial detector samples the distribution after the velocity of the laboratory has been added \cite{Evans2019_SHMRefined}. 
Each velocity class contributes one term to the multimode field in Equation~\eqref{eq:ALP_sum}, with
\begin{equation}
    \nu(\mathbf{v})
    =\frac{m_a c^2}{h}+\frac{m_a v^2}{2h},
    \qquad
    \mathbf{k}(\mathbf{v})=\frac{m_a\mathbf{v}}{\hbar}
    \,.
\end{equation}
The continuous velocity distribution therefore maps directly onto a continuous frequency distribution.

The phases of the occupied modes are assumed to be uncorrelated. 



Their sum is a random walk in the complex $\Psi$ plane: the two quadratures approach Gaussian random variables, while the instantaneous field envelope follows a Rayleigh distribution.
As modes with different kinetic energies accumulate relative phase, both the envelope and phase change stochastically \cite{Centers2021Stochastic,Gramolin2022Feb_SpectralSignatures}.
The field is approximately a coherent oscillation only on times short compared with its coherence time.

For a local halo density $\rho_{DM}$, the r.m.s.\ Rabi frequency $\Omega_a$ can be computed from Equations~\eqref{eq:ALP_field}, \eqref{eq:a0_rhoDM}, and~\eqref{eq:HaNN} \cite{Centers2021Stochastic}:
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_a
    = \dfrac{1}{2\hbar} g_{\mathrm{aNN}} a_0 m_a c v_{\bot}
    \approx \dfrac{1}{2} g_{\mathrm{aNN}}
    \sqrt{2\hbar c \rho_{DM}}\,v_{\bot}
    \,,
    \label{eq:Omega_a_MW}
\end{equation}
%\AW{this is a stochastically varying quantity!}
Here we assume that the ALP-field gradient comes exclusively from the relative motion of the field and the detector.\footnote{This is not necessarily the case, for instance, for ALPs gravitationally bound in stationary states like axion stars or ``axion atoms'' \cite{Lam2017_PhysRevD, Liang_Zhitnitsky2019_GravitationallyPhysRevD}.
We do not consider such regimes here.}

If we take $v_{\bot} = \SI{220}{\km\per\second}$, we have $\Omega_a/2\pi=g_{\mathrm{aNN}}\times\SI{0.2}{\GeV\cdot\Hz}$.
For an experiment searching for $g_{\mathrm{aNN}}\leq 10^{-10}\,\SI{}{\per\GeV}$, the Rabi period is $2\pi/\Omega_a \geq 1000\,\mathrm{yr}$; the ALP-field gradient is therefore a weak drive on the spins.


\subsection{Coherence time}\label{sec:theory:MWALP:coherence}  % for MW axion

For a Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution with average speed $v_a \approx \SI{220}{\km\per\s}$ \cite{Evans2019_SHMRefined}, the ALP field spectral linewidth (FWHM) observed by a detector on Earth is
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma_a = \dfrac{\nu_a}{Q_a}
    \,,\label{eq:Gamma_a_Qa}
\end{equation}
where $Q_a \equiv (c/v_a)^2 \approx 1.8\times10^6$ is the ALP quality factor.
The ALP coherence time $\tau_a$, defined by $\Gamma_a = 2\pi/\tau_a$, is therefore
\begin{equation}
    \tau_a = \dfrac{2\pi Q_a}{\nu_a}
    \,.
    \label{eq:taua}
\end{equation}
Within one coherence time, the superposition in Equation~\eqref{eq:ALP_sum} is well approximated by the coherent mode in Equation~\eqref{eq:ALP_field}.
On longer time scales, the random phases between modes cause the amplitude and phase of the effective oscillation to drift, so the field must be treated as a stochastic process \cite{Centers2021Stochastic}.
For Compton frequencies above \SI{1}{\kHz}, $\tau_a \lesssim \SI{1000}{\second}$; the interplay between $\tau_a$ and the NMR relaxation times determines the optimal measurement strategy in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}.




\subsection{Periodic modulations}\label{sec:theory:MWALP:modulations}

% sensitive axis of CASPEr-G
CASPEr-G is only sensitive to the component of $\boldsymbol{\nabla}a$ perpendicular to its sensitive axis. 
In the resonance scheme, the sensitive axis is aligned with the bias field $\mathbf{B}_0$.
Since the bias field is always veritcal with repect to the ground, the setup is sensitive to gradient along $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}$ directions, but not to the gradient along $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ direction.

% \subsection{Daily modulation}

The ALP-field gradient driving spin precession depends on the direction of the relative velocity between the Earth and the local dark-matter halo.
As the Earth rotates, this direction sweeps through space with a period of one sidereal day.
An ALP signal therefore exhibits a daily modulation of its amplitude and phase \cite{Gramolin2022Feb_SpectralSignatures}.

% \subsection{Annual modulation}

In addition to daily rotation, the Earth's orbital velocity around the Sun modulates the relative ALP-detector velocity with a period of one year.
Both the daily and annual modulations have precisely predictable periods and phases, making them useful consistency checks for any claimed dark-matter-candidate signal \cite{Gramolin2022Feb_SpectralSignatures}.
